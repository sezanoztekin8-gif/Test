# Packaging: titles and thumbnails

Title and thumbnail work as one unit. The thumbnail grabs attention, the title
adds the context or the twist. Neither should simply repeat the other.

## Title formulas (slots in brackets)

| Formula | Example shape |
|---|---|
| Curiosity gap | `Why [Familiar Thing] Is Actually [Unexpected Quality]` |
| Specific result | `How I [Achieved Specific Result] in [Timeframe]` |
| Warning | `Stop [Common Action] Until You Know [Thing]` |
| Hidden truth | `The [Adjective] Truth About [Topic]` |
| Story | `The [Person/Thing] Who [Did Something Surprising]` |
| Question | `What Happens If [Scenario]?` |
| List | `[Number] [Things] That [Outcome]` |
| Contrast | `[Cheap/Old/Small] vs [Expensive/New/Big] [Thing]` |
| Challenge | `I Tried [Thing] for [Time]` |
| Explainer | `[Topic] Explained in [Time/Simple Terms]` |
| Ranking | `Every [Category] Ranked from [Worst] to [Best]` |

Make formulas fit the language: word order, length and what reads as natural
differ by language. Don't translate English formulas literally.

## Title rules

- Front-load meaning: the first ~40-60 characters must work alone.
- Specific beats vague ("in 30 days" beats "fast").
- Emotion or tension beats description ("The mistake that..." vs "About mistakes").
- 1-2 emphasised words max; avoid all-caps titles and repeated punctuation.
- Honest: deliver it early in the video; disappointment costs future reach.

## Thumbnail principles

- One focal point, readable on a phone (test at ~160 px wide).
- Faces with clear emotion work in many niches; characters, objects or scenes
  work when faces don't fit (faceless, animation, documentary, products).
- 0-4 words of text, large, high contrast, adding information.
- Strong contrast between subject and background; avoid busy backgrounds.
- Create a question: before/after, unusual scale, an object out of place, danger, mystery.
- Consistent style helps returning viewers recognise the channel, but don't let
  consistency make every thumbnail look the same.
- Check the feed context: what do competitors' thumbnails for the same topic look
  like? Stand out in colour and composition.
- Avoid misleading imagery; it hurts satisfaction signals and can break policy.

## Describing a thumbnail formula

Write it as parts a viewer could point at:
`[subject, emotion, position] + [secondary object/element] + [text, colour, position] + [background]`
e.g. `[character, shocked, right third] + [glowing object, oversized, left] + ["NOT AGAIN" white, top left] + [dark blurred scene]`.

## Testing

Use YouTube Studio's Test & compare for thumbnails (and titles where offered).
Test one big difference at a time. Judge on watch time share/CTR for that
video, not on other videos.
